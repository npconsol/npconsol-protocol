    How matching works

    Shape alone proves nothing. A string that looks like an address is either equal to a stated register value or it is unverified. When the contract record flips to stated, the slot at the top fills in and this console starts recognising it.

    03 / Method

    Three habits.
    Zero guesses.

    The page follows a short routine so that nothing unproven ever reaches the screen as fact.

    01

    Read the register

    Each outside fact sits in one record with a state: stated, absent or unconfirmed. Only a stated record is allowed to show a value or a link.

    02

    Match, never assume

    Anything shaped like an address is compared with stated values. A look-alike with no match is flagged as unverified, whatever its prefix.

    03

    Follow the tape

    Market rows come straight from a public DEX feed and keep their own rhythm, separate from the contract slot and from every other record.

      Privacy

      What this page touches.

      No accounts, no cookies, no forms that travel. The sections below spell out the few moving parts.

      Your browser

      Everything you type into the console stays in the tab. The page keeps no profile of you and writes nothing to storage.

      Market requests

      Your browser asks a public DEX interface for token data. That provider, and any network between you and it, can see ordinary connection details such as your IP address.

      Clipboard

      Copy buttons place exactly the address shown beside them on your clipboard, and nothing else.
